Where I'm from, chak-chak is a go-to sweet to buy in the supermarket and bring with you when you are invited to someone's house. But where I currently reside, it is not the case at all. 
Thus, my only option is to cook it myself, and since I don't do it too often I decided to document the process for the next time.
 <img src="/my-blog/cooking/images/chak-chak.jpg" />
 
If you want to know more about chak-chak, check out the [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chak-Chak) page.
## Ingridients
So, to make this sticky but very tasty desert, you will need:
**For the "noodles"**
- 2-3 eggs
- 200-300g of flour (the amount of flour depends on the size of the eggs and the overall humidity of the environment)
- a pinch of salt
- half of a (small) teaspoon of baking soda or baking powder
- oil for frying (the amount of oil depends on the size of the pot you are using for frying)
**For the syrup**
- 150g of sugar
- 150g of honey
- 2 table spoons of water
**Optional**
- 100g of nuts (I prefer wallnuts) and or raisins
  
## Preparation
### Noodles
Mix the eggs, salt, around 200g of flolur and baking soda together, then continue to add flour and mix until the dough reaches a firm consistency that doesn't stick too much to the hands, but still is elastic enough, so that if you form a ball out of it and press your finger in it, you should be able to make a dent with not too much pressure and the dough should partially recover after you lift your finger. Leave the dough to rest under a kitchen towel or plastic wrap while you prepare the surface to roll out the dough.

Put a little flour on the rolling surface, then place the dough on it (you can split the dough in several parts if you feel it is more convinient). Put a little flour on top and roll the dough to 0.3-0.5cm thickness. Lightly flour the rolled dough and cut it in strips of 3cm width. Now comes the tricky part: put the strips on top of one another and cut the "block" in 0.5cm matchsticks. This step might actually prove rather difficult, especially if the dough is on the more humid side, so after the first cut, check if you can separate the matchsticks between each other. If not, you can separate the dough strips again, then let them dry a little more and add a bit more flour before assembling them again or you can cut strips separately (which I find more tedious than just waiting a bit). 

Now, that you have your matchsticks, prepare for the deep frying. You are about to cook with very hot oil, so make sure that the pot and the skimmer or strainer
(or if you have a deep fryer, that thing) are perfectly dry before the oil touches them. Put enough oil in your pot, to be able to comfortably fish out the fried noodles with your weapon of choise. Then heat the pot. When you put the first noodle in there, it should start sizzling and move around a bit and it will increase in volume quickly, then when you turn it in 10-15 seconds, the fried side should have a nice slightly golden color. Now you are ready to fry the rest of the noodles in small baches, turning them half way until they all have this slightly golden color. Put the fried noodles somewhere, where the residual oil can drain. Really try to not overdo it, the resulting sticks should not be too brown for the nice presentation. Also note, that there will be a lot of flour around the raw noodles, and you should try to the best of your ability to shake it off before the noodle goes into frying (or the flour will burn in the oil and stick to your beautiful fried noodles).

### Syrup
Mix together honey, sugar, and a bit of water and put it on a stove. Get this mixture to boil (again, this thing will be extremely hot! I know, it's tempting to try sweet things in the cooking process, but don't). It's going to go in bubbles, so if it's boiling too much, you can reduce the heat, just so it is a little above the boiling point. In a few minutes, when the syrup has a nice golden but not yet brown color, take a cup with cold water and put a little drop of the syrup in it. Pick the syrup drop with your hands, you should be able to roll a little ball with it. This way you know the syrup is ready. At this moment turn off the heat and pour the syrup in the fried noodles. Be careful, at this point the syrup is still very hot! This is also the moment to add cruched nuts or raisins if you want. 

Mix everything well and form a hill of fried noodles in the syrup on a plate, or put the mix in a baking form (just know, it will stick in there). Press ligthly (you can press with your hands, wet them so they don't stick too much). Now let chak-chak rest for a couple of hours at room temperature and enjoy!

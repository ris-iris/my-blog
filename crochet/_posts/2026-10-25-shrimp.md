So, this is how you crochet a shrimp:

# Head
The head part is very simple, it's standard amigurumi increases every second row:
1. Magic ring, 6 sc
2. 6 inc
3. 12 sc
4. (1 sc, 1 inc) x6
5. 18 sc
6. (2 sc, 1 inc) x6
7. 24 sc
8. (3 sc, 1 inc) x6
9. 30 sc
10. 30 sc
11. 30 sc
12. 30 sc
13. 30 sc
14. 30 sc
15. 30 sc

# Legs
To crochet the leg together with the body, just chain 6 and then do 5 single crochet back in place of a single crochet

<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_leg_chain.webp" />
<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_legs_crochet.webp" />

16. 1 leg, 5 sc, 1 leg, 25 sc
17. 30 sc
18. 30 sc
19. 1 leg, 5 sc, 1 leg, 25 sc
20. 30 sc
21. 30 sc
22. 1 leg, 5 sc, 1 leg, 25 sc
23. 30 sc

As kindly clarified by my biologist boyfriend, shrimps belong to the order of Decapoda and, as the name implies should actually have 10 legs. If you want to crochet a biologically accurate shrimp, you should start adding legs starting straight from row 10.

# Body turn
Rows 24 and 34 here are made to mimich the shell segments overlay, you can skip it if you don't want it. To do it, you should crochet only in the front loop of the stitch

<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_front_loop.webp" />
<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_overlay.webp" />

24. 7 sl st, (front loop only) 23 sc 
25. 7 sl st, (back loop of the prev. row) 5 sc, 2 hdc, 9 dc, 2 hdc, 5sc
26. 7 sl st, 5 sc, 1 sc2tog, 9 dc, 1 sc2tog, 5sc
27. 7 sl st, 5 sc, 1 hdc, 1 dc2tog, 5 dc, 1 dc2tog, 1hdc, 5sc
28. 7 sl st, 5 sc, 1 hdc, 7 dc, , 1hdc, 5sc
29. 7 sl st, 5 sc, 1 hdc, 1 dc2tog, 3 dc, 1 dc2tog, 1hdc, 5sc
30. 7 sl st, 5 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, , 1hdc, 5sc
31. 7 sl st, 5 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, , 1hdc, 5sc

The skip is a bit unconventional, but it is simple to do, just take your loop and hook it through the stich after the skip

<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_skip_begin.webp" />
<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_skip_finish.webp" />

32. skip 7, 4 sl st, 1 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1 hdc, 1 sc, 4 sl st
33. skip 7, 4 sl st, 1 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1 hdc, 1 sc, 4 sl st
34. skip 7, (front loop only) 17 sc

Then, you'll need to cover this skipped rows, by crocheting over it using the last not skipped row, and each row after that with front post crochets (crochet over the post of the row before the skips, not the stich itself)

<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_fp.webp" />

35. 7 sc, (back loop of the prev. row) 5 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1hdc, 5 sc
36. 7 FPsc, 5 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1hdc, 5 sc
37. 7 FPsc, 4 sl st, 1 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1hdc, 1 sc, 4 sl st
38. 7 FPsc, 4 sl st, 1 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1hdc, 1 sc, 4 sl st
39. 7 FPsc, 4 sl st, 1 sc, 1 hdc, 5 dc, 1hdc, 1 sc, 4 sl st
40. 24 sc
41. 13 sc, 1 sc2tog, 1 sc, 1 sc2tog, 6 sc
42. 1 sc2tog, 3 sc, 1 sc2tog, 15 sc
43. 10 sc, 1 sc2tog, 1 sc, 1 sc2tog, 5 sc
44. 5 sc, 1 sc2tog, 9 sc, 1 sc2tog
45. 5 sc, 1 sc2tog, 7 sc, 1 sc2tog
46. 5 sc, 1 sc2tog, 5 sc, 1 sc2tog
47. (1 sc2tog, 1 sc) x 4
48. 4  sc2tog

# Tail
Now we do two tail details, both starting attaching to the body as we go. These details are not crochet in a circle, just going back and forth and attaching each next row to the body going away from center before turning your work.
<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_tail_chain.webp" />
<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_tail_connect.webp" />

1. 7 ch (from the last free loop of the body)
2. 6 sc, attach to the body
3. 6 sc, 2 ch
4. 7 sc, attach to the body
5. 6 sc
6. 6 sl st

<img src="/my-blog/crochet/images/shrimp_tail.webp" />

# Final touches

I completely forgot to add the whiskers while I was doing the head, so now is the time: attach the yarn to the head where the whisker should be, chain 16, then do 6 inceases and then 10 sc, repeat on the other side.

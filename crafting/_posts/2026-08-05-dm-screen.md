Sometimes I get very ambitious and curious at the same time and decide to try a new technique on an important project. This time it resulted in a DM screen that was made as a birthday gift. So, here is how it went...

## Design
After careful consideration of what I would most likely need and how much time it will take to get shiped, I ordered 6 pieces of basewood (30x21 cm), wood glue, wood wax, varnish, some hinges and an utility knife and started brainstorming how exactly to assemble the DM screen that would be functional and aesthetic at the same time. I wanted the screen to convert into a box for storing the dice, maps, miniatures and other things the DM might need whent it is folded. So that all roleplaying materials are in one place and it is easy to transport. And I wanted the box compartment to be on the left, so that it is not getting in a way of DMing. At the same time, I wanted a uniform and symmetric look from the players' side. It took me some time but in the end I landed on the design that you see here

## Construction

After cutting the smaller panels and splitting the right wide pannel, the first few hinges were not that hard to screw in.

<img src="/my-blog/crafting/images/dm_screen_3.jpg" />
<img src="/my-blog/crafting/images/dm_screen_4.jpg" />



The challenge was to screw the hinges on the right pannel, so they are not too visible from the player's side. For that I glued additional pieces of basewood at the places where hinges would be screwed in to get the right thickness. This is the time I should have actually thought about how to screw self-tapping screws in between two pieces of wood glued together and realised I should have made the grooves for the screws in advance. But I didn't, so when I started screwing them in, I broke off the pieces that were glued and had to make the grooves and glue them back. After a long time strugling with this process, I got decent ''hidden'' hinges.

Then I constructed the ''box'' for the left pannel, and cut out the shape of the folded right pannel on the top and the bottom box walls which turned out satisfyingly precise for the first woodworking project.

Then I sended the box walls edges at a 45 degree angle and glued and screwed the box walls to the base left panel.

## Coating and decoration

Unfortunately, I had to disassemble everything at this stage to sand, varnish and coat with wax. Additionally, I glued fabric corners on the center and right pannels, so that DM can pus pieces of paper in them. 

<img src="/my-blog/crafting/images/dm_screen_12.jpg" />
<img src="/my-blog/crafting/images/dm_screen_16.jpg" />

And after assembling everything again I realised that I have no way of removing the screws ends that were sticking out from the players' side, so I decided to cover them with pieces od cardbord soaked in wood glue.
